The psychology & neuroscience curriculum

Inspired by university learning

Week one · Foundations

Discover How The Mind Works

In your first module, you will learn what psychologists do. You will get to grips with investigative methodologies and understand the difference between an observation, an assumption and a conclusion supported by evidence. You will also explore why research involving people must be designed with care and respect, and the different career paths that Psychology can lead you down.

Can we trust our senses? This module explores how the brain interprets the world around it through concepts including visual illusions, attention and the way expectations shape what we notice. You will engage in real perception exercises to discover differences between how yourself and your classmates see the world. You will then explore why individuals may see the world differently.

Why can you remember a song from years ago but forget something you heard this morning? In this module, you will explore how memories are formed and recalled and conduct your own investigations into attention, learning and recall. You will apply the results of your experiments to create an effective study plan.

Week two · Put ideas into practise

Understand Yourself And Others

This module explores how the brain and nervous system help us to respond to the world around us. You will explore what happens to the brain on a biological level when different emotions are experienced, and consider how factors including sleep quality and stress impact mood.

You will kick off Week 2 by considering how groups influence the choices we make. You will analyse how friendship, family relationships, cooperation, peer pressure and other social factors impact our thoughts and actions through case studies and hands-on activities. Bringing your learning into the real world, you will discuss when it helps to follow a group and when it is better to think independently.

This module explores developmental psychology, a branch of the science which investigates how people learn, navigate relationships and develop new skills throughout their lives. You will examine the techniques used by developmental psychologists and interrogate how experiences in childhood and adolescence impact the way adults see the world.

How do you think screens impact your behaviour? This module first looks at the link between changing attention spans and the use of digital technology. You will also examine how online claims and the content we absorb impacts our own thoughts, feelings and behaviours. You will evaluate the similarities and differences between the human mind and computer processes such as machine learning. Finally, you will learn how psychologists communicate their findings.

Faculty & speakers

Taught by leading academics and practising psychologists

Academy Royale International’s Psychology & the Human Mind course is taught by an expert faculty of researching academics and working psychologists.

Dr Jack Andrews

University of Oxford

Dr Jack Andrews is a Senior Research Fellow and Wellcome Early-Career Fellow in the Department of Experimental Psychology at the University of Oxford. He leads the Social Mental Health Lab, where his research focuses on adolescent mental health, peer relationships and school-based interventions, with work featured by major international media outlets, including the BBC, The New York Times, Forbes and The Independent.

Archana Ramesh

University of Oxford

Archana Ramesh is a DPhil researcher in Clinical Neurosciences at the University of Oxford. Her doctoral research focuses on epilepsy genetics, rare neurodevelopmental disorders and the use of clinical and genomic data to improve diagnosis. She also has experience across genomic medicine teaching, clinical research coordination and science communication.

Dr Stuart Clare

University of Oxford

Dr Stuart Clare leads the core team at the Oxford University Centre for Integrative Neuroimaging, supporting research across MRI, electrophysiology, brain stimulation, histology and wearable technologies. Working across three departments, he oversees a multidisciplinary team of around 30 staff helping advance brain imaging research and its clinical applications. He holds a PhD from the University of Nottingham.

Dr Anna Scarnà

University of Oxford

Dr Anna Scarnà is a psychologist and neuroscientist at the University of Oxford with expertise spanning language, personality and psychological disorders. Her wide-ranging research has explored bilingual language, dopamine in psychiatric disorders and trauma in chimpanzees. She teaches neuroscience and psychology at Oxford and is the author of two books on primatology and trauma.

Experimental Psychology Summer School FAQs

Everything families need to know

Detailed answers for parents and students comparing Psychology & Neuroscience summer schools.

The course is designed for bright, ambitious students fascinated by the workings of the human brain and enthusiastic to learn more about the science of psychology. Students don’t need to be committed to a degree or career path related to Psychology, they just need to show enthusiasm and willingness to learn.

No previous knowledge is needed. Most of our students encounter Psychology for the first time on our course, so it is designed as an in-depth introduction to this fascinating subject. All students need is an enthusiasm for scientific learning and an interest in the course.

The course is designed to push bright, curious students to face new academic challenges with confidence. It challenges their thinking by introducing complex concepts and ethical problems. Practical challenges consolidate their learning, and students are encouraged to try their best to achieve their academic goals. Their learning is guided by expert members of our faculty and takes place in small classes, ensuring that every student feels supported and enjoys their learning while encountering new ideas.

Most students in this age group will not yet be considering university choices, but some may be thinking ahead to their futures already. For these students, the course is excellent preparation for studying Psychology or a related subject at university. Students gain skills and knowledge which they would not have ordinarily encountered at school, putting them in a strong position to craft a compelling application. Students have the opportunity to ask their tutors and industry professionals for application advice. They also receive a certificate, grades and a report which make excellent supporting materials for a strong university application.

The course is very hands-on. Students are encouraged to apply their learning to the real world through practical exercises including participating in and designing real Psychology experiments. This makes sure that students’ learning is brought into the real world, showing them what a future in Psychology might look like.

A typical morning involves a lecture and two seminars or workshops. In the afternoon students have another extended workshop or practical session. The rest of the day is spent socialising with friends during activities, enjoying delicious meals in inspiring campus settings and exploring the city.

The programme takes place in the inspiring campus of the University of Cambridge. Students have access to university-level facilities and teaching spaces.

By teaching in small groups, we ensure that each student has a personalised relationship with their tutor. This means they receive high-quality constructive feedback and any support they need in class. It also means that students bond with their classmates, creating a friendly atmosphere in which students get involved and ask questions with confidence.

Our students come from all over the world. Since our first summer school in 2004, we have welcomed students from over 175 countries. They speak a wide range of languages and come from a wide range of backgrounds, creating a vibrant community perfect for international students.

At the end of the course, students receive a graduation pack which contains a certificate acknowledging their academic achievements during the course. They also receive a personalised report and detailed grades.

Medicine insights

A handy guide for students considering Psychology & the Human Mind at Academy Royale International and beyond

Is Psychology & the Human Mind the right subject for me?

Psychology & the Human Mind is an exciting and fast-moving field which combines scientific study with ethics and healthcare concerns. Ask yourself these questions to determine if Psychology & the Human Mind is the right path to follow.
  • Am I curious about why I behave the way that I do?
  • Am I empathetic towards the causes of other people’s behaviour?
  • Do I want to get hands-on with my learning by experimenting, debating and presenting projects?
  • Have I got a passion for mental health awareness?

What will I learn?

At an Academy Royale International Summer School, we demonstrate how the scientific background of Psychology and experiments in the field are applied in the real world to help people understand themselves and others.
  • Core strands of Psychology including development, memory and social thinking
  • The ethical considerations of psychological experimentation
  • How to conduct and present the findings of a research experiment
  • How the biology of the brain influences the field

How should I choose a location?

Each of our locations delivers serious academic study within an inspiring campus. Choose your perfect location according to the setting, atmosphere and dates which suit you best. These are some questions to ask yourself when choosing.
  • Would I prefer the busy atmosphere of Oxford or the more peaceful and scholarly atmosphere of Cambridge?
  • Which dates on offer fit with the rest of my summer plans?
  • Which city am I the most excited to explore with an international community of new friends?
