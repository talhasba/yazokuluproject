What lawyers learn

Immersive learning and courtroom experience

Week one · Foundations

Branches of the Law

In this introductory module, you will critically explore the importance of law in society by examining how laws provide structure, order and fairness. You will also consider the consequences of a lawless society. Focusing on the UK legislative system but thinking comparatively in a global context, you will examine how laws are made from proposal to enforcement. Finally, you will consider the rule of law, the idea that nobody (including the government) is above the law, and consider the impact of this principle on society.

This module considers the relationship between the state and citizens, and how laws protect values including justice, fairness and equality. By debating fascinating case studies, you will explore how morality and ethics interact with lawmaking, considering whether all laws are fair and what happens when the law fails to protect the most vulnerable in society.

Looking globally, this module considers how human rights are enforced and why they matter for everyone in society. You will examine the institutions tasked with protection human rights internationally and look to history to understand the development of the concept of human rights, and how they have been treated in different countries across different time periods. Again, you will turn to case studies to analyse real-world examples.

This module trains you to think like a lawyer. You will take part in workshops to improve your public speaking skills and learn the courtroom procedures and techniques which make a successful lawyer. You will also explore alternative dispute resolution, a method which aims to resolve cases before they make it to trial. These skills will be put into practice at the end of the course in your mock trial.

Week two · Your Future in Law

Find Your Place in Court

This module begins by examining how crimes are defined, prosecuted and defended through criminal law. You will explore comparative criminal law, examining how certain crimes are prosecuted differently according to the legal systems and cultural norms of different nature. You will conduct an in-depth case study into the different prosecutions of murder between countries. In a simulated murder case, you will take on many roles in the system including police and lawyers to deliver justice, while gaining an understanding of the processes of evidence collection and argumentation.

In this module you will look at civil law, the other major branch of the law. You will be introduced to contracts and tort law, examining how agreements are made and what happens when they are broken. Finally, you will develop your critical thinking skills by comparing the legal systems and approaches of different countries and cultures.

This exciting module is your chance to take to the courtroom floor as you participate in the mock trial Challenge Day, presenting cases, examining witnesses and deliberating as a jury. This is an exciting opportunity to experience life as a lawyer in a realistic courtroom environment.

This module introduces you to various potential careers open to law graduates, as well as the pathways into law which do not necessarily involve a law degree. It is an opportunity to reflect on what you have learned about yourself and your own interests during the programme. You will also examine the changing influence developing technology has on the law, and the ways that interaction with the media can influence the legal system.

Learning outcomes

By the end, you will be able to:

You will leave Academy Royale International with a new understanding of what lawyers do, and the skills they need to do it.

You will have gained a strong background in the major areas of the law, and the career opportunities within each one.

The mock trial Challenge Day is a chance to put everything you have learned about the UK legal system into practice. You will engage in complex argumentation and cross-examination to reach a verdict and sentence on a compelling case, all while following proper courtroom etiquette.

  • The role and importance of the law in society, covering concepts of structure, order and fairness
  • The process of law-making in the UK, including proposal in Parliament and enforcement in practice
  • Similarities and differences between UK and international legal systems and principles, including the structure of legal systems and the interactions between cultural values and the law
  • What human rights are and how institutions work to protect them
  • How crimes are defined, prosecuted and defended
  • Civil law including contract and tort law
  • How the courtroom works in practice

Law Summer School FAQs

Everything families need to know

Detailed answers for parents and students comparing Law summer schools.

This programme is designed for any students who are enthusiastic and interested in the law. Students do not need to be aspiring lawyers, they just need to be interested in law and society. On the course, they will gain a range of insights and skills which will benefit them across a range of subjects including politics, history and business.

No previous subject knowledge is needed. This course provides a strong foundation in the study of law as well as introducing and developing new skills. Thanks to the small class sizes and regular opportunities for personalised feedback, students feel supported to excel while they encounter new academic challenges.

The course is designed to push bright students to take on new academic challenges by introducing lots of new concepts and practical tasks. While students are stretched, their learning is guided by expert members of our faculty and takes place in small classes, ensuring that every student feels supported and enjoys their learning while encountering new ideas.

Many students in this age group will not have started thinking about university, but for those who leave Academy Royale International set on going to law school, the course is the perfect early preparation for university applications. Our students have a head start on their knowledge of the law and the opportunity to ask their tutors and real law professionals for application advice. They also receive a certificate, grades and a report which make excellent supporting materials for a strong university application.

The course is very hands-on. Each module involves practical demonstrations and tasks, and the final Practical Project is a chance for students to immerse themselves in an extended piece of hands-on work. Students enjoy consolidating their learning through these practical challenges and getting to know their classmates during team challenges.

A typical morning involves a lecture and two seminars or workshops. In the afternoon students have another extended workshop or practical session. The rest of the day is spent socialising with friends during activities, enjoying delicious meals in inspiring campus settings and exploring the city.

The programme takes place in one of two inspiring campuses in the historic colleges of the Universities of Oxford and Cambridge. Whichever location they choose, students have access to university-level facilities and teaching spaces.

By teaching in small groups, we ensure that each student has a personalised relationship with their tutor. This means they receive high-quality constructive feedback and any support they need in class. It also means that students bond with their classmates, creating a friendly atmosphere in which students get involved and ask questions with confidence.

Our students come from all over the world. Since our first summer school in 2004, we have welcomed students from over 175 countries. They speak a wide range of languages and come from a wide range of backgrounds, creating a vibrant community perfect for international students.

At the end of the course, students receive a graduation pack which contains a certificate acknowledging their academic achievements during the course. They also receive a personalised report and detailed grades.

Law & Justice Insights

A helpful guide for students considering Law & Justice at Academy Royale International and beyond

Is Law & Justice the right subject for me?

Law is an interesting and academically challenging subject to study, straddling lots of areas of subject knowledge and involving empathetic engagement with some challenging topics. Ask yourself these questions to determine if Explore Law is the right subject for you.
  • Am I passionate about making sure people receive justice?
  • Do I want to experience real courtroom procedure and improve my public speaking skills?
  • Have I got the enthusiasm needed to engage in this broad and dynamic subject?
  • Do I often wonder about how the laws in my country compare to other nations?

What will I learn?

At an Academy Royale International Summer School, we give you a foundation in the core knowledge of your subject alongside the skills needed to apply it in the real world. You will leave with a new appreciation for the importance of law in society and your potential future within it.
  • The skills and qualities of a lawyer, as well as the range of jobs that a law degree can lead to
  • How public law mediates the relationship between people and states
  • The importance of human rights and the work of institutions to protect them
  • Core concepts and practical applications of criminal and civil law

How should I choose a location?

Each of our locations delivers serious academic study within an inspiring campus. Choose your perfect location according to the setting, atmosphere and dates which suit you best. These are some questions to ask yourself when choosing.
  • Would I prefer the busy atmosphere of Oxford or the more peaceful and scholarly atmosphere of Cambridge?
  • Which dates on offer fit with the rest of my summer plans?
  • Which city am I the most excited to explore with an international community of new friends?
